Strategy notes for the AI commander. Edit freely: re-read at every planning pause. The player's notes plus lessons
from recorded battles. (The previous version is kept in old/strategy.2026-11-09.md.)

TEMPO
- Contact is not the goal; the objective is. By minute 10 name the objective to take or hold and write the attack
  plan. Commit the freshest brigades to it by minute 20 and the reserve by minute 30. Still losing the exchange at
  minute 60 with no objective in hand: stop trading, concentrate on one point and attack it, or fall back onto an
  objective you hold.
- Fire at the edge of fire range only to shake the enemy so that a charge succeeds; never trade volleys there for
  many turns. Battles are decided by charges on shaken units.

SCOUT, FORM, SHOOT, CHARGE, EXPLOIT
1. Scout first: skirmishers and cavalry ahead, the main body 500+ behind. If they find an unexpected force or battery,
   halt and adjust before committing.
2. Form one continuous line in "Line" formation on the FRONT LINE PLAN slots, as wide as the visible enemy front,
   flanks on woods, rivers or hills, before contact. Skirmishers screen in front, then go to the flanks once the main
   line engages. Artillery behind the infantry, on high ground if possible.
3. Advance and fire. Walk; run only for the last part of a charge, to reach a key spot or to escape.
4. Charge a target that is shaken, reduced, exhausted, already engaged, exposed on a flank, or the weakest in reach
   (CHARGE OPPORTUNITIES), with at least two units, ideally one frontal and one on the flank, while others keep firing.
5. Exploit: when a unit routs or the line breaks, push fresh units through the gap towards the enemy rear; pursue
   routers with fresh cavalry only.

CHARGING AS THE SOUTH
- Southern infantry and cavalry are good in close combat: between one more volley and a charge on a shaken enemy,
  charge. Not every unit is superior: judge experience, morale, fatigue and numbers.
- Do not charge a fresh, steady, supported unit on open ground, nor one in fortifications or dense forest: weaken it
  with fire and artillery, hit its flank, or charge a weaker neighbour. Repeaters and entrenchments make frontal
  charges worse: prefer flanks, rear and local superiority.

CONCENTRATION AND SUPPORT
- 2 to 3 units on one enemy unit, spread along the line so several enemies are shaken at once. Pin part of the enemy
  with fire while the strongest brigades hit a weak or exposed sector.
- Keep about one fifth of the infantry back early to reinforce, close a gap or exploit; commit it as the fight is
  decided. A unit that has held or fired in range for several turns must charge, advance or be rotated out.

CAVALRY AND ARTILLERY
- Sabre cavalry: isolated, shaken, surprised or retreating troops and exposed batteries, on the flank or rear.
  Carbine cavalry: scouts, screens, harasses, can fight dismounted from cover, avoids long melee.
- Batteries: enemy artillery, formed brigades and units about to be charged; concentrate them on important targets;
  never fire into friendly troops in melee; always covered by infantry.

MORALE AND CONDITION
- Rotate exhausted, shaken or depleted units behind the line and replace them; preserve survivors. Exploit a wavering
  enemy at once: fire, then charge. Keep the general behind the line to rally wavering units.

TERRAIN
- Anchor and shorten the line on ridges, woods and obstacles. Cross rivers at fords and bridges, never under fire;
  hold your bank and strike enemy groups that cross alone. Use fortifications in defence; do not assault entrenched
  troops frontally again and again: concentrate fire on one section and go round the flank.
- Keep a garrison on objectives you hold; take unheld ones in reach with local superiority; do not split the army
  across several objectives.

DISCIPLINE
- Let an order finish before changing it unless the situation changed (rout, threatened flank, breakthrough, new
  enemy force). No back and forth: every move has a purpose. Withdraw in order before units are surrounded.
