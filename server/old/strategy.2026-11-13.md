Strategy notes for the AI commander. Edit freely: re-read at every planning pause. The player's notes plus lessons
from recorded battles. (The previous version is kept in old/strategy.2026-11-09.md.)

TEMPO
- Contact is not the goal; the objective is. Time is counted as a share of the phase's time (TEMPO). By 10 % of it
  name the objective to take or hold and write the attack plan. Commit the freshest brigades to it by 25 % and the
  reserve by 40 %. Still losing the exchange at 70 % with no objective in hand: stop trading, concentrate on one
  point and attack it, or fall back onto an objective you hold. (Without a battle timer: minutes 10, 20, 30, 60.)
- With the enemy near the objective (visible and seen recently, see TEMPO in the ASSAULT PLAN) under a third of your
  strength, launch the assault group at once; do not wait for the rear brigades or the batteries. In woods the
  enemy you see is rarely all the enemy there is: two brigades alone are not an assault.
- Watch the clock every turn (TEMPO, TIME LEFT, CLOCK): a phase objective not taken by the end of the timer is a
  lost battle, however well the line is formed. If the march to the point takes longer than the time to the launch
  deadline, you are behind schedule: march now, form the line on the way, and let the late brigades join.
- An assault is a march to firing positions close to the defenders (cover first: wheat, woods' edge,
  fortifications), a few volleys to shake them, then charges. Go for the WEAK POINT of the defence (lowest morale,
  experience and support), from its flank when you can: the server stages the assault group there, fires from the
  best covered spots in range, and charges shaken defenders, any target it has outflanked, and steady ones when two
  or more fresh brigades good in melee are in reach. Give the rest of the army orders that feed the assault:
  batteries and skirmishers on the weak point, cavalry on its rear.
- Fire at the edge of fire range only to shake the enemy so that a charge succeeds; never trade volleys in the open
  for many turns. Battles are decided by charges: on shaken units, into flanks, and by fresh brigades together.

COMMAND
- You command the army; the server is your staff. Decide which objectives to take and in what order, which
  brigades assault each one ("assaults" in your answer), from which side, and when they go in. With an aggressive
  doctrine and two objectives within reach, two assault groups at once beat one group after the other. Keep one
  brigade in reserve, the batteries on the fixing fire targets and the cavalry on the weak side.

SYNCHRONISED ASSAULTS AND THE LAST THROW
- Two assaults launched together force the player to split his attention: same objective from two sides, or two
  objectives at once. The server waits until both are ready.
- With 20 minutes left and the objective still the enemy's, everything fresh goes in: close to charge distance,
  then all charge at once. Prepare it: batteries on the defenders, cavalry on their flank, fresh brigades kept
  fresh for it.

COHESION
- Brigades fight together. Never send one brigade alone far from the army into ground that holds no objective:
  only scouts (cavalry, skirmishers) go out alone. Do not chase skirmishers or cavalry with a line brigade: they
  fall back faster than it can follow and lead it into a trap. The server stops such chases and brings isolated
  brigades back.

SCOUT, FORM, SHOOT, CHARGE, EXPLOIT
1. Scout first: skirmishers and cavalry ahead, the main body 500+ behind. If they find an unexpected force or battery,
   halt and adjust before committing.
2. Form one continuous line in "Line" formation on the FRONT LINE PLAN slots, as wide as the visible enemy front,
   flanks on woods, rivers or hills, before contact. Skirmishers screen in front, then go to the flanks once the main
   line engages. Artillery behind the infantry, on high ground if possible.
3. Advance and fire. Walk; run only for the last part of a charge, to reach a key spot or to escape.
4. Charge a target that is shaken, reduced, exhausted, already engaged, exposed on a flank, or the weakest in reach
   (CHARGE OPPORTUNITIES), with at least two units, ideally one frontal and one on the flank, while others keep firing.
5. Exploit: when a unit routs or the line breaks, push fresh units through the gap towards the enemy rear; pursue
   routers with fresh cavalry only.

CHARGING AS THE SOUTH
- Southern infantry and cavalry are good in close combat: between one more volley and a charge on a shaken enemy,
  charge. Not every unit is superior: judge experience, morale, fatigue and numbers.
- Do not charge a fresh, steady, supported unit on open ground with one tired brigade, nor one in fortifications or
  dense forest: weaken it with fire and artillery, hit its flank, or charge a weaker neighbour. A mass charge of
  fresh brigades (morale 0.8+) good in melee can break it. Repeaters and entrenchments make frontal
  charges worse: prefer flanks, rear and local superiority.

CONCENTRATION AND SUPPORT
- 2 to 3 units on one enemy unit, spread along the line so several enemies are shaken at once. Pin part of the enemy
  with fire while the strongest brigades hit a weak or exposed sector.
- Keep about one fifth of the infantry back early to reinforce, close a gap or exploit; commit it as the fight is
  decided. A unit that has held or fired in range for several turns must charge, advance or be rotated out.

CAVALRY AND ARTILLERY
- Sabre cavalry: isolated, shaken, surprised or retreating troops and exposed batteries, on the flank or rear.
  Carbine cavalry: scouts, screens, harasses, can fight dismounted from cover, avoids long melee.
- Batteries: enemy artillery, formed brigades and units about to be charged; concentrate them on important targets;
  never fire into friendly troops in melee; always covered by infantry.

MORALE AND CONDITION
- Rotate exhausted, shaken or depleted units behind the line and replace them; preserve survivors. Exploit a wavering
  enemy at once: fire, then charge. Keep the general behind the line to rally wavering units.

TERRAIN
- Anchor and shorten the line on ridges, woods and obstacles. Cross rivers at fords and bridges, never under fire;
  hold your bank and strike enemy groups that cross alone. Use fortifications in defence; do not assault entrenched
  troops frontally again and again: concentrate fire on one section and go round the flank.
- Garrisons guard far objectives against a raid round the front; size them by the force ratio and the ambition
  (OBJECTIVES advice): a cavalry or skirmisher picket when attacking or badly outnumbered, one steady brigade on a
  far objective the result depends on when defending. Never split the main effort across the objectives.
- To take an objective a unit must stand on the point itself, inside the capture range, for several minutes.

WITH THE NUMBERS
- Use the superiority early: take the objective the player needs while it is unheld or weakly held, with a group
  1.5 times its defenders, from two sides at once. Trading fire while the enemy concentrates on the point wastes it.

OUTNUMBERED AND LOSING
- Do not feed brigades back into the fire one at a time: the player concentrates on each in turn. Break contact
  with the whole line behind a rearguard, rest out of reach, come back together, or keep the army for the next
  battle when this one is lost.

LOSING THE EXCHANGE
- Attack from the woods early if the plan says so; once the kill ratio turns against you and casualties count,
  go back into the woods and make the enemy come to you across the open ground.

OUTNUMBERED
- If the strength ratio is under 0.5 at minute 20 and the assault group has not engaged, cancel the assault for the
  rest of the battle and defend the objectives held; do not re-plan an assault until the ratio exceeds 0.8. With a
  casualty ratio among the goals, every lost assault also loses that condition: defend, and count the men.
- Defending outnumbered: one line on good ground covering the objectives, batteries behind, wagons close, a reserve
  to relieve; give ground rather than brigades. A brigade captured is worth more to the enemy than the ground.

DISCIPLINE
- A unit on the march keeps its move: no new move unless the destination changes by more than 200 (the server drops
  it). Every new move makes the unit stop, turn and start again.
- Let an order finish before changing it unless the situation changed (rout, threatened flank, breakthrough, new
  enemy force). No back and forth: every move has a purpose. Withdraw in order before units are surrounded.
